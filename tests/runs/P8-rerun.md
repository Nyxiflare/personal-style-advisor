Congratulations on your granddaughter! I'd be glad to help you look and feel lovely on the day.

You're right that most clothes are a struggle. They are cut for people standing up, not for people

A few quick questions so I don't guess:

1. Where do you live, and where will the ceremony be? (town and country)

## Assistant

Thank you. Here is my advice.

**In short**
- **The look:** a ready-stitched off-white kasavu saree with a peacock green blouse.
- **The colours:** off-white and soft gold, with peacock green near your face.
- **The one fit tip:** everything opens at the front and fastens without small hooks, so your carer
  can dress you sitting down.

**First, about clothes being a struggle**

You can wear a saree again. A ready-stitched (pre-stitched) saree has the pleats sewn in and a waist
that closes like a skirt. Your carer can put it on you while you sit, in a few minutes. Many tailors
can also turn a saree you already own into a ready-stitched one. For the blouse, ask the tailor for
a front opening with large flat buttons, magnetic buttons or a soft hook-and-loop strip instead of
small hooks. Put the right (weaker) arm into the sleeve first, and take it out last.

**Your reading, updated**
- **Rich, clear colours:** now *Medium confidence*. You feel more yourself in strong colours, and you
  love peacock green. Both point the same way.
- **Warmth of your skin and gold vs silver:** still *unconfirmed*. So I've built your colours near
  the face from the ones that work for both likely colour groups: peacock green, deep emerald,
  maroon and deep plum. Off-white kasavu is your tradition, so it stays.
- Everything else is as before.

If you'd like to check, there is a two-minute test you can do sitting by a window: your carer holds
an off-white cloth and then a bright white one under your chin, then a gold chain and a silver one,
and tells you which makes your skin look smoother and your eyes brighter. It's optional.

**For the ceremony and lunch** (daytime, humid, at home then a hall)

**Look 1: Ready-stitched kasavu saree** (my first choice)
- *What:* soft cotton kasavu saree, off-white with a **thin** gold border, ready-stitched. Peacock
  green cotton blouse, elbow sleeves, round or soft V neck, front opening, lined with soft cotton.
- *Sitting details:* the waist closes with elastic or a hook-and-loop strip at a comfortable seated
  height, not tight. Ask the tailor to cut the front slightly longer and the back slightly shorter
  so the hem sits evenly, and to keep the hem clear of the footrests. Keep the pallu short and light,
  pinned at the shoulder and again at the waist, so it can't reach the wheels.
- *Why it suits you:* the saree look you miss, without pleating; peacock green near the face gives
  the contrast your silver hair and dark eyes carry well; cotton for humid Kochi.

**Look 2: Ready-to-wear set-mundu**
- *What:* off-white mundu with a thin gold border and a matching neriyathu, both cotton. The mundu
  waist made with elastic or hook-and-loop. The neriyathu pinned over the shoulder, not tucked. The
  same peacock green blouse, or a deep emerald or maroon one.
- *Why it suits you:* lighter and simpler than a saree, very Kerala, very graceful.

**Look 3: Easiest of all, kurta and dupatta**
- *What:* a peacock green cotton kurta that opens fully at the front, cut a little shorter at the
  front so it doesn't bunch on your lap. Soft straight pants with an elastic waist and no back
  pockets or thick seams. A short off-white kasavu stole pinned at both shoulders.
- *Why it suits you:* nothing to drape, quickest for your carer, and still has the kasavu touch.

**For all three**
- *Sensitive skin:* choose cotton kasavu with a thin border, not heavy zari. Ask for the blouse and
  any border that touches your skin to be lined with soft cotton. Before buying, rub the border on
  the inside of your wrist: if it scratches there, it will scratch at your waist.
- *Holding the baby:* no brooches or pins on the front. Put pins at the shoulder and side. Choose
  smooth jewellery with no sharp edges, and soft fabric where the baby's face will rest.
- *Jewellery:* a short, smooth necklace and small studs. Gold suits the kasavu border, though your
  best metal isn't confirmed yet. Bangles on the left wrist only, or openable bangles if your right
  hand is sore. Skip anything you have to squeeze over your knuckles.
- *Hair:* a soft low bun suits silver hair and keeps it off your neck in the heat. Jasmine flowers if
  you enjoy them.
- *Feet:* soft slip-on sandals or ones with a hook-and-loop strap. Your feet are on show on the
  footrests, so a neat pair is worth it.
- *Heat:* cotton all the way, a small handkerchief, and a light stole in case the hall air
  conditioning is cold.
- *Photos:* sit facing the window or the main light. The peacock green and the gold border near your
  face will help the camera see you clearly.

**Culture check:** off-white and gold kasavu is the usual festive dress for a Malayali Hindu family
event. It's worth a quick word with the family in case they have a colour plan for the day, or
special clothes for the ceremony itself.

**Budget (about ₹5,000):** first ask your carer to check what you already own. An old kasavu saree
can be made ready-stitched, which saves the most. If buying, spend most of it (about half to
two-thirds) on the saree or set-mundu, about a fifth on the blouse and tailoring, and the rest on
small things like sandals, pins or flowers. Tailors usually need about a week or more, so book early
once you know the date.

## Remaining defects

1. **Three question rounds are still the minimum before any advice (SKILL.md, "Pace" and Step 4).**
   Step 1, Step 2 and the mandatory Step 4 check-in each need their own turn when the opening message
   gives almost nothing, as here. For an older user who said "most clothes are a struggle", three
   rounds is heavy. *Change (SKILL.md, Pace section):* add "Light path: for words-only users who seem
   tired, older, or in a hurry, ask the Step 4 memory questions inside the Step 2 round (e.g. white vs
   off-white, gold vs silver, bold vs dusty), then show the readings at the top of the advice turn,
   mark the unanswered ones unconfirmed, and invite correction. This counts as the check-in." Mirror
   it in the checklist line "The check-in happened (or readings are marked unconfirmed)".

2. **Greying guidance still contradicts the deep-skin method (body-proportions.md section 12 and
   face-framing.md "Greying" bullet).** Both still say grey and silver hair "often" moves a person
   toward "softer or cooler" palettes. For medium-deep or deep skin, silver hair raises contrast and
   usually supports clearer, richer colour near the face, which is what this run concluded by
   judgement. *Change:* replace both bullets with "Greying changes contrast: on light skin it lowers
   contrast (often softer colours suit); on medium-deep and deep skin it raises contrast (strong,
   clear colour or a crisp border near the face usually suits). Re-check with two questions: do
   bright colours now look better or louder than before, and does black near the face look sharp or
   heavy?"

3. **Step 1 still lists more asks than the Pace rule allows (SKILL.md Step 1 vs "Pace").** Step 1
   has four compound items plus "what they already own", "words, photos or both", and an open
   heritage question; with gender that is seven or eight asks against "about four". This run had to
   drop "what you own" from turn 1 and fold heritage into Q3. *Change (Step 1):* mark the order:
   "Ask first: place, budget, the event (date and venue), outfit or tradition in mind, and gender if
   unclear. Ask later, only if needed: what they own (ask in the advice turn's budget section), photos
