## 1. How sure can we be?

Face-shape labels are fuzzy. In published classifiers, accuracy drops when more overlapping classes
are used (IdentiFace, arXiv 2401.01227: about 94% with three classes vs about 88% with five, with
heart and oval the weakest). An Inception v3 study (arXiv 1911.07916) reported about 84-85% on 500
celebrity images, while simpler classifiers scored about 36-65%. Real faces are blends, and hair,
angle and lens distortion change the outline. So:

- Give face shape as a **blend** with **Medium** confidence at best from photos, **Low** from words.
- Advise from **features** (what is widest, how long, how angular) so the advice holds even if the
  label is wrong.
- Phone front cameras held close widen the nose and narrow the jaw. Ask for the camera at arm's
  length or a back camera at about 1.5 m.

## 2. Reading by features

Ask, or read from a straight-on photo with hair pulled back:
1. Which is widest: forehead, cheekbones or jaw? Or are they about equal?
2. Face length compared with cheekbone width: about 1.5 times or more (long), about 1.3 (balanced),
   or about equal (short)?
3. Jawline: soft and rounded, or angular with visible corners? Chin: pointed, rounded or square?
4. Feature scale: small and delicate, medium, or large and bold (eyes, nose, lips, brows)?
5. Neck: shorter, average or longer. Shoulders: sloped or square.

## 3. Face families and blends

| Family | Feature pattern | Framing goal | Usually works | Use with care |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oval / balanced | length ~1.3-1.5× width, cheekbones slightly widest, soft jaw | keep balance | most necklines, frames and haircuts | — (choose by personality and scale) |
| Round / short-soft | length close to width, soft jaw, full cheeks | add length and some angles | V and U necks, longer earrings, angular or upswept frames, height or side parts in hair | very round frames, chin-length rounded bobs, chokers if neck is short |
| Square / short-angular | wide angular jaw, forehead ≈ jaw | soften corners, or embrace the strength | scoop, sweetheart, cowl, round or oval frames, soft layers, hoops | boxy frames as wide as the jaw, blunt cuts ending at the jaw |
| Oblong / long | length clearly greater than width, forehead ≈ cheek ≈ jaw | add width, shorten | boat, square and crew necks, wide frames with strong brow line, volume at the sides, fringe/bangs, studs and short drops | long narrow pendants, very long straight hair with centre part, tall height on top |
| Heart / inverted triangle | wide forehead and cheekbones, narrow pointed chin | add width at the lower face | sweetheart, scoop, boat necks, bottom-heavy or rimless frames, chin-length or longer layers, chandelier and triangle earrings | heavy top volume, top-heavy frames |
| Diamond | cheekbones clearly widest, narrow forehead and jaw | balance the cheekbones | oval or cat-eye frames, fringe, volume at the jaw, studs and drops | very narrow frames, hair tight at the cheekbones |
| Triangle / pear | jaw wider than forehead | add width on top | browline and cat-eye frames, volume at the crown, open necklines | jaw-length blunt cuts, wide jaw-level earrings |

Blends: describe both ("oval with a squarer jaw") and take advice from the stronger feature.

## 4. Necklines and blouse necks

| Goal | Western necklines | South Asian blouse / kurta necks |
|---|---|---|
| Lengthen a short neck or round face | V, deep scoop, open collar, sweetheart | V-neck, deep U, sweetheart, boat neck with low back, collarless (Chinese collar only if neck is long) |
| Widen a long neck or narrow shoulders | boat, square, crew, cowl, off-shoulder (if modesty allows) | boat neck, square neck, high Chinese/band collar, halter (if they want it) |
| Balance broad shoulders | V, scoop, halter, raglan sleeve | V or U neck, sweetheart; avoid very wide boat necks |
| Soften a strong jaw | scoop, sweetheart, cowl | round or sweetheart neck |
| Cover (modesty) | high round, mock neck, band collar, layering under a cardigan | high neck, band (Chinese) collar, collared blouse, full sleeves; wear the dupatta or pallu over the shoulder |

Blouse backs (dori tie, keyhole, deep U) are optional and modesty-dependent. Always ask first.

## 5. Collars and lapels (shirts, jackets, kurtas, sherwanis)

- Collar points should echo or balance the face. Spread or cutaway collars widen a long face;
  point collars lengthen a round face. Button-down collars read casual.
- Band collars (mandarin, Nehru, bandhgala) suit longer necks best. With a short neck, keep the band
  low (about 2.5-3 cm) or wear it slightly open.
- Lapel width should be in proportion to the person's frame and chest (narrower lapels on fine
  frames, medium to wide on broad frames).
- Kurta and sherwani collars: band collar is standard. A V-placket or open neckline lengthens a
  shorter neck.

## 6. Earrings and necklaces

- **Length**: long drops and ear-to-shoulder chandeliers lengthen. Studs, short hoops and ear cuffs
  keep the line short and suit long faces and long necks.
- **Shape**: angular (geometric, triangle, kite) adds definition to soft faces; curved (hoops,
  teardrops, jhumkas) softens angular faces.
- **Jhumkas and chandbalis**: bell jhumkas widen the lower face (good for heart and long faces).
  For a short neck, choose smaller jhumkas and skip the heavy choker when wearing them.
- **Necklaces**: a choker shortens the neck (good for long necks). Long rani haar and pendants
  lengthen (good for short necks and round faces). For layering, the top layer should sit at or
  above the neckline and the next ones follow its shape.
- **Metals**: follow the person's colouring (see `colour-analysis.md`). Gold, silver and mixed metals
  are all fine when they match the person's best temperature. Temple, kundan, polki, oxidised silver
  and pearls each have their own mood (traditional, bridal, boho, classic).

## 7. Glasses

Rules, in order:
1. **Fit first**: the frame width matches the face width at the temples; the eyes sit near the
   middle of each lens; the brow line sits at or just below the eyebrows; no pinching at the nose.
   Low-bridge or Asian-fit nose pads suit many East and Southeast Asian faces and anyone with a low
   or flat nose bridge.
2. **Contrast the face shape**: round faces → rectangular, square, browline or cat-eye; square →
   round, oval, rimless; long → tall lenses, wide frames, decorative temples; heart → bottom-heavy,
   oval, light rims; diamond → oval, cat-eye, rimless.
3. **Scale**: bold features and deep colouring can carry thick, dark frames; fine features and low
   contrast suit thin metal, clear or soft-tone frames.
4. **Colour**: frame colour from their palette. Warm colouring: tortoiseshell, honey, olive, gold.
   Cool colouring: black, grey, navy, burgundy, silver, clear-grey. Muted colouring: soft
   tortoiseshell, taupe, gunmetal. High contrast: black or crystal-and-black.
5. **Strong prescriptions**: smaller lenses reduce edge thickness for high minus lenses. A
   full-rim frame hides edge thickness. Mention high-index lenses as an optician question.

## 8. Hair

- **Add length** (round, short faces): height at the crown, side parts, long layers past the chin,
  face-framing pieces that start below the cheekbone.
- **Add width** (long faces): volume at the sides, fringe/bangs, waves, chin- or shoulder-length
  cuts.
- **Soften angles** (square faces): layers, waves, side-swept fringe, avoid blunt cuts at the jaw.
- **Balance a narrow chin** (heart faces): fullness at jaw level, side-swept fringe.
- **Texture**: work with natural texture (straight, wavy, curly, coily). Coily and curly hair gives
  natural volume; shape it (tapered cuts, defined curls, braids, locs, twists) rather than suggesting
  straightening. Protective styles are styles in their own right.
- **Hair colour**: stay within one or two levels of the natural colour for the most natural result,
  and match the colouring's temperature (warm: golden, copper, caramel; cool: ash, burgundy,
  espresso). Henna and mehendi give warm red-orange tones.
- **Greying**: silver or white hair changes contrast, and the direction depends on skin depth. On light skin it lowers contrast and often moves the palette softer or cooler; on medium-deep and deep skin it raises contrast and supports clearer, richer colour near the face. Re-check with two questions: does crisp white or cream look better now, and do bold colours look more alive or louder than before?
- **Men and short hair**: a taper or fade with some length on top lengthens round faces; side
  volume or a textured crop suits long faces; a beard can add jaw definition (see below).

## 9. Headwear, hijab, turban, dupatta and scarf framing

The colour next to the face matters most. A head covering is the face-framing colour, so choose it
from the person's best face colours.

- **Hijab**: draping can shape the face line. Volume at the sides and a softer drape widen a long
  face; a closer wrap with the fabric meeting below the chin and a slight point lengthens a round
  face. Use matte jersey or chiffon for soft drape, structured crepe for crisp lines. An underscarf
  (inner cap) in a skin-near or palette neutral keeps lines clean. Never suggest removing or
  loosening a hijab for styling.
- **Turban and pagri (Sikh dastar, Rajasthani safa, wedding pagri)**: the colour frames the face.
  Matching or contrasting the pagri with the groom's or family colours is common at weddings; the
  shape and wrap style are religious and regional, so ask and don't redesign them.
- **Dupatta and pallu**: worn over one shoulder, both shoulders, over the head, or pinned at the
  shoulder. A dupatta draped from both shoulders frames the face and adds width (good for narrow
  shoulders). A single-shoulder or diagonal drape lengthens. A head-covering drape (ghoonghat, for
  gurdwara, mosque, temple or family custom) puts the dupatta's border colour right at the face, so
  choose a border from the person's palette.
- **Scarves (Western)**: a long loop or open drape lengthens; a short knot or snood widens.
- **Caps and hats**: wider brims balance long faces; height in the crown (fedora, trilby) lengthens
  round faces; scale the brim to the shoulders.

## 10. Beard and grooming

- **Round face**: keep the sides shorter and add length at the chin (a short boxed beard, goatee or
  anchor) to lengthen.
- **Long face**: fuller sides and a shorter chin; avoid long pointed beards.
- **Square face**: a rounded or shorter beard softens; stubble keeps the strong jaw visible.
- **Heart face**: a fuller beard at the chin and jaw balances a wide forehead.
- **Neckline of the beard**: about two fingers above the Adam's apple. Cheek line natural or
  slightly lowered.
- Some people grow beards for religious reasons (for example many Sikh and Muslim men). Then only
  advise on grooming and fit with clothing (collars, turban, colours), never on shaping that
  conflicts with their practice.
- **Eyebrows**: groomed brows frame the eyes for everyone; their natural shape is usually the right
  starting point.

## 11. Scale

Match the scale of prints, jewellery, buttons, bag size and frame thickness to the person's feature
scale and frame (small, medium, large). Small features and a fine frame are overwhelmed by giant
prints and chunky necklaces. Bold features and a broad frame make tiny prints look busy and lost.
Height matters less than bone structure and feature size.
