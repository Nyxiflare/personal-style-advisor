# Regional and traditional wear

Written 2026-11. Customs vary by family, caste, religion, region and generation, and change over
time. Treat everything here as "common practice", ask about the person's own family and community,
and never present one region's custom as the rule for a whole country. Saree-drape sources disagree
on details (for example the direction of the Gujarati Seedha Pallu), so where they differ this file
says so.

## Part A. India

### A1 Sarees: drapes by region

| Drape | Region | How it sits | Suits / when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nivi | Andhra origin; the pan-India default | waist wrap, front pleats tucked at the navel, pallu over the left shoulder (pleated and pinned, or open) | everyone; office, weddings, parties; open pallu for drama, pleated pallu for neat and petite looks |
| Bengali (aatpoure) | West Bengal, Tripura | broad box pleats or no front pleats, pallu brought round and over the left shoulder again; traditionally a key bunch tied to the pallu end | Durga Puja, weddings; red-bordered white (laal paar sada) for Puja and Bijoya |
| Gujarati / Seedha Pallu | Gujarat, Rajasthan, parts of MP and UP | pallu comes from behind over the right shoulder and spreads across the front to show the border (sources differ on the exact path) | bridal (panetar, gharchola), showing an embroidered or bandhani pallu; widens the upper body visually |
| Nauvari / Kashta | Maharashtra | nine yards, tucked between the legs at the back like a dhoti, giving trouser-like freedom | Maharashtrian weddings, Gudi Padwa, Lavani; Paithani silk with a green or purple body is classic |
| Madisar | Tamil Nadu (Brahmin communities; Iyer and Iyengar styles differ) | nine yards, kachham tuck at the back, pleats and a waist knot | weddings and religious ceremonies for married women in those communities; ask before suggesting |
| Mundum Neriyathum (Set-mundu) | Kerala | two pieces: mundu at the waist, neriyathu over the blouse and tucked; off-white with gold kasavu border | Onam, Vishu, weddings; Kerala bridal (Hindu) often in kasavu |
| Kodava (Coorgi) | Kodagu, Karnataka | pleats at the back, pallu brought from back over the right shoulder and pinned; matching head scarf for brides | Kodava weddings and festivals |
| Mekhela chador | Assam | two or three pieces: mekhela (skirt) and chador (wrap); muga, eri or pat silk | Bihu, weddings |
| Phanek and innaphi | Manipur | wrapped sarong (phanek) with a shawl (innaphi) | festivals, weddings |
| Kunbi / Goan | Goa (Kunbi community) | tied over one shoulder, often red check | heritage events; modern Goan Christian weddings often use Western gowns |
| Odia, Kannadiga (Ilkal), Telugu (Gochi kattu) drapes | Odisha, Karnataka, Andhra and Telangana | regional variants of front-pleated and kachha drapes | ask for family style |
| Modern drapes | pan-India | pre-stitched or ready-to-wear saree, dhoti saree, pant saree, belted saree, saree with jacket or cape, lehenga saree, butterfly pallu (very narrow pleats) | petite (pre-pleated keeps volume low), wheelchair users (pre-stitched), first-time wearers, cocktail and sangeet |

Drape tips by proportion:
- **Petite**: narrow pleats, light fabric (georgette, chiffon, soft silk, light Kanjivaram), slim or no
  border, pleated pallu, pallu ending at mid-thigh, petticoat and saree reaching the floor in heels.
- **Tall**: wide borders, heavy silks, open pallu, Gujarati drape, contrast blouse.
- **Wider hips than shoulders**: crisp fabrics with flat pleats, structured blouse with detail, Seedha
  Pallu or open pallu to add upper width.
- **Wider shoulders**: soft fabrics, deep V or U blouse, pleated pallu kept close.
- **Fuller midsection**: tuck the petticoat slightly above the navel, use a soft-drape fabric, keep
  pleats flat, choose a longer blouse (to the waist) or a jacket blouse.

### A3 Blouses

Necklines (link to `face-framing.md` for face and neck rules):
- Round / jewel: classic, pairs with heavy necklaces; shortens a short neck slightly.
- U and deep U: lengthens; shows a long necklace or a haar.
- V and deep V: lengthens most; balances broad shoulders and round faces.
- Sweetheart: softens strong jaws, frames the collarbone, bridal favourite.
- Boat (bateau): widens narrow shoulders and long necks; elegant with Kanjivaram.
- Square: frames angular collarbones; good with chokers.
- High neck / Chinese (band) collar: modest, regal; suits long necks; good for petite when blouse and
  saree share a colour.
- Collared (shirt collar): modern, office, Indo-western.
- Halter, off-shoulder, cold-shoulder, sleeveless, corset: modern options; only if the person wants
  them and the setting allows.

Backs: deep U, keyhole, dori (tie) with tassels, potli buttons, sheer back, closed back. Ask first
for anything open.

Sleeves: cap, elbow (most versatile), three-quarter (elegant, warmer), full (modest, winter,
bridal), puff and bell (fashion). Longer sleeves balance broad upper arms only if the person wants
that; it is never required.

Blouse length: standard ends under the bust; a longer blouse (to the waist) or a jacket blouse
covers more, suits pregnancy, cooler weather or modesty.

### A4 Kurtas and suits

Cuts: straight, A-line, Anarkali (fitted bodice, flared skirt), kalidar (panelled flare), angrakha
(overlapping wrap), kaftan, high-low, asymmetric, front-slit, jacket-style, Pathani (men's, collar,
shirt-tail hem), Lucknowi (chikankari), short kurti.

Lengths and what they do:
| Length | Effect | Pairs with |
|---|---|---|
| Waist / crop | shortens the torso; shows the waist | lehenga, sharara, dhoti pants, high-rise palazzo |
| Hip | casual, youthful | jeans, cigarette pants |
| Thigh (short kurta) | everyday; lengthens legs for long-torso, short-leg bodies | straight pants, leggings, jeans |
| Knee | the versatile default | churidar, straight pants, palazzo, salwar |
| Calf / below knee | elegant, lengthens tall frames; on petite frames pair with slim bottoms and same-colour | churidar, cigarette pants, sharara |
| Ankle / floor (Anarkali, gown kurta) | formal, dramatic | churidar, leggings |

Bottoms: churidar (gathers at the ankle, lengthens), salwar and Patiala (volume; Punjabi), palazzo
(fluid, hides nothing and needs nothing hidden: it simply moves), straight pants / cigarette (modern,
office), dhoti pants (fusion), sharara (flared from the knee) and gharara (flared from the knee with a
gathered joint; Lucknow and Hyderabadi Muslim bridal tradition), lehenga skirt.

Slits: side slits that start at the hip ease movement and lengthen the line; high slits are modern
and need straight pants beneath.

### A5 Lehengas and ghagras

| Silhouette | Shape | Suits | Care with |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-line | gentle flare from the waist | most bodies; petite (choose lighter fabric) | — |
| Flared / circular (kalidar) | full circle, many panels | tall, any frame; dancing (sangeet, garba) | heavy fabric on petite frames |
| Panelled | vertical panels | lengthens; petite | — |
| Mermaid / fishtail | fitted to the knee, flared below | defined waist and hips; long frames | hard to sit and dance in |
| Sharara-lehenga, lehenga saree | lehenga with pre-draped pallu | sangeet, cocktail; first-time saree wearers | — |
| Straight / column | narrow fall | petite and minimalists | — |
| Trail lehenga | train at the back | bridal, reception | walking and stairs |
| Tiered / layered | horizontal tiers | tall | petite (adds width; use fewer tiers) |
| Jacket / cape / crop-top lehenga | Indo-western tops | sangeet, cocktail, modern | modesty (choose longer tops or jackets) |

Can-can (net petticoat layers) adds volume; choose less can-can for petite and seated wearers.
Blouse (choli) lengths range from crop to waist; longer cholis and jacket tops add coverage.
Weight: bridal lehengas can be heavy, so ask about the venue, heat and how long the person will stand.

### A6 Dupatta styling

- **One shoulder, long fall**: classic, lengthens, keeps hands free.
- **Both shoulders (U in front)**: frames the face, widens narrow shoulders, modest.
- **Over the head**: bridal, religious places (gurdwara, mosque, some temples), family custom; choose
  the border from the person's best face colours.
- **Pinned at one shoulder, falling behind**: sangeet and dancing; shows the choli.
- **Cape or criss-cross**: modern; defines the waist when belted.
- **Saree-style dupatta**: tucked at the waist and over the shoulder with a lehenga.
- **Double dupatta (bridal)**: one on the head, one over the shoulders.
- **Belted**: for Indo-western; defines the waist.
- **Wheelchair users**: pin the dupatta at both shoulders or use a short stole; avoid long fabric near
  wheels.

### A8 Wedding and festival colour customs (common practice; ask the family)

Brides:
- North Indian Hindu and Sikh: red, maroon, pink or other rich colours; pastels are now common too.
- Bengali: red Banarasi, with white conch and red coral bangles (shakha-pola) for married women.
- Tamil: Kanjivaram in red, maroon, mustard, green or other colours with contrast borders; Brahmin
  families may use a nine-yard madisar for the main rites.
- Telugu: often white or cream silk with a red border for the muhurtham, then a coloured silk.
- Kerala Hindu: off-white kasavu with gold; Kerala and Goan Christians: white saree or white gown.
- Maharashtrian: green or yellow Paithani, often in a Nauvari drape; green glass bangles.
- Gujarati: panetar (white with red) and gharchola (red with a bandhani grid).
- Muslim (many communities): red, green, gold or pastel lehenga, gharara or sharara; walima often pastel.

Guests:
- Avoid competing with the bride: skip full bridal red and the heaviest bridal lehengas.
- Many Hindu families avoid all-black and all-white at the wedding ceremony itself; receptions in
  cities are more relaxed. Ask.
- Silk sarees and silk veshtis are the norm for South Indian ceremonies; men's white or off-white
  veshtis are standard.
- Events and their usual moods: **haldi** yellow and light cottons (they will get stained);
  **mehendi** greens, florals, comfortable clothes for sitting; **sangeet** bright, dance-friendly,
  Indo-western; **ceremony** traditional silks; **reception** jewel tones, glamour, bandhgala or
  sherwani, saree or lehenga or gown.

Festivals (common customs): Diwali (bright, gold, new clothes); Durga Puja (red-and-white, new
clothes for each day); Navratri (nine colours, one per day, published each year in Maharashtra, and
chaniya choli or kediyu for garba); Onam (kasavu); Pongal and Sankranti (silk, new clothes); Holi
(old white clothes); Eid (new clothes, often white or pastel, kurta pajama, sharara); Christmas in
Goa and Kerala (Western or saree); Baisakhi and Lohri (phulkari, bright suits, kurta pajama); Bihu
(mekhela chador, muga); Teej (green, leheriya); Karva Chauth (red, bridal-style). White is widely
the colour of mourning in Hindu families.

### A9 Fabrics by climate and occasion

| Climate | Good fabrics | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Humid heat (Chennai, Kolkata, Mumbai, Kochi) | cotton, mulmul, silk-cotton, Chettinad cotton, linen, chanderi, light georgette, soft silk | heavy brocade and velvet for daytime, polyester satin |
| Dry heat (Rajasthan, Delhi summer) | cotton, kota doria, linen, mulmul, chiffon; loose covering shapes for sun | dark heavy fabrics at noon |
| Monsoon | quick-dry synthetics blends, chiffon, crepe; shorter hems (ankle) | white sheer cotton, suede, heavy silk |
| North Indian winter weddings | velvet, brocade, raw silk, heavy Banarasi, pashmina and jamawar shawls, thermal layers under | bare sheer fabrics without a shawl |
| Hill stations / cold | wool, pashmina, layered silk, long coats over sarees | — |

Occasion weight: office (cotton, linen, light silk, handloom), day events (silk-cotton, chanderi,
organza), evening (silk, georgette, velvet), bridal (heavy silk, brocade, zardozi).

### A10 Jewellery and footwear

- Styles: temple (gold, deity motifs; South Indian classical), kundan and polki (uncut stones;
  North Indian bridal), meenakari (enamel), jadau, oxidised silver (boho, casual), pearls (classic,
  Hyderabadi), Kolhapuri saaj (Maharashtra), mangalsutra (married women in many communities),
  maang tikka, matha patti, nath (nose ring), jhumkas, chandbalis, bangles (glass, gold, lac).
- Match metal to colouring (see `colour-analysis.md`); scale to features and frame (see
  `face-framing.md`).
- Footwear: juttis, mojaris, kolhapuris, block heels, wedges; wear in advance for long events.

## Part I. North America

- Western wear (USA, Canada): denim, boots, hats. Black American church and celebration traditions
  (Sunday best, hats, coordinated colours). Hawaiian aloha shirts and muʻumuʻu (Hawaiʻi).

## Part K. Wearing another culture's dress respectfully

- When hosts invite guests to wear their dress (an Indian wedding, a Korean hanbok rental tour, a
  Nigerian aso ebi), wearing it is usually welcomed. Ask the host which pieces and colours.
- Buy from makers in that community when possible; rent for one-off events.
- Don't wear sacred or earned items (religious headwear as costume, Indigenous headdresses,
  ceremonial cloaks) unless they were given or the community allows it.
- Learn the name of what you wear and how to wear it properly (drape, tie, wrap direction).
- **Diaspora and mixed heritage**: people with roots in more than one tradition often wear pieces from
  each day to day (an ankara overshirt with Irish linen trousers, a kurta with jeans, a hanbok-inspired
  jacket at work). That is their own culture, not borrowing. Ask which traditions they want to draw
  on and how visible they want them to be; never assign a tradition from their appearance.
