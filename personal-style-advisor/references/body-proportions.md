## 1. Language rules

The goal is fit, comfort and a look the person chose, never hiding or shrinking.

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| hide your tummy / thighs / arms | skim, give room, drape over, choose a fabric that moves |
| flaws, problem areas, trouble spots | areas where fit is tricky; where clothes usually pull |
| slimming, makes you look thinner | lengthens the line, defines the waist, balances shoulders and hips |
| flattering for your size | suits your proportions |
| you can't wear X | X works best when…; if you love X, try it with… |
| apple, pear (as a label for the person) | "your shoulders and hips are about the same width" |
| fair skin is better / brightens you / too dark | your colouring is deep and warm; these colours echo it |
| normal size vs plus size (as better/worse) | straight sizes, plus sizes (as sizing ranges only) |
| wheelchair-bound, suffers from | wheelchair user, has |
| that's fixable, we'll correct your colouring, no brightening needed | the right colours near your face will make the difference |

If the person uses negative words about their own body, don't repeat them. Acknowledge the
feeling, then give practical options for the outcome they want.

## 2. Why proportions, not types

Single-photo body-type tools are unreliable: one single-image body-shape paper reported 71.4%
accuracy, with errors linked to camera angle (arXiv 2305.18480). A Japanese photo-based frame
tool (Luft Skeletal Camera) says its results change a lot with camera height, angle, pose and
clothing. Kibbe app reviewers often found manual selection more reliable than photo analysis.
So work from a few **proportions** the person can measure or describe, and give confidence.

## 3. What to measure or ask (all optional)

Words work fine. Measurements help with fit, never ask for weight.
- Height.
- Shoulder width vs hip width: about the same, shoulders wider, or hips wider.
- Waist definition: clearly narrower than shoulders and hips, a little, or straight.
- Torso vs legs: where the hip bone sits compared with the middle of the body. A quick test: stand
  against a wall and compare crown-to-hip-crease with hip-crease-to-floor. If legs are clearly longer,
  the person has a short torso; if clearly shorter, a long torso. Or ask "do trousers usually need
  hemming, and do tops ride up or look long?"
- Frame: wrist and bone size (fine, medium, broad), and whether ready-to-wear shoulders fit.
- Where clothes usually pull, gape, ride up or feel tight.
- Seated users: measure seated (waist, hip, thigh, inseam and back rise).

## 4. Proportion goals and how to reach them

| What the person has | Common goal | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulders wider than hips, and they want to **show** it (common for athletic builds) | define the V | athletic-fit shirts with waist darts, fitted (not tight) knits, tapered trousers, structured jackets with a defined waist, bandhgala or Nehru jacket cut close at the waist, enough armhole and upper-arm room |
| Shoulders wider than hips | balance top and bottom | V and scoop necks, raglan or dropped sleeves, darker or plainer tops, volume or detail below (A-line, wide-leg, flared lehenga, Anarkali flare, straight kurta with palazzo), avoid heavy shoulder pads |
| Hips wider than shoulders | balance | boat or square necks, structured shoulders, detail and lighter colour on top, straight or A-line skirts, bootcut or straight trousers, kurtas that fall past the hip, saree pleats kept flat with a well-fitted petticoat |
| Similar shoulders and hips, little waist definition | define or keep straight on purpose | wrap shapes, belts at the natural or high waist, peplums, or embrace straight shapes (column dresses, straight kurtas, shift dresses, straight-cut suits) |
| Defined waist, balanced shoulders and hips | show the waist | wrap, fit-and-flare, belted, tailored kurta with side slits, fitted blouse with saree; avoid boxy oversized shapes unless that's the chosen style |
| Fuller midsection | room and length | empire or high waist, wrap and surplice shapes, structured open jackets, longer kurtas with straight panels, stretch waistbands, avoid tight waistbands right on the middle |
| Short torso, long legs | balance | low or natural waist, longer tops untucked, single colour on top, avoid very high waists |
| Long torso, short legs | lengthen legs | high-rise trousers and skirts, tucked or cropped tops, same colour from waist to shoe, heels or platforms if wanted, shorter kurtas (hip or upper-thigh) with straight pants, sarees draped a little higher |
| Long torso and long legs (tall overall), long arms | proportion and length | check sleeve and shirt-tail length first (long-arm or "tall" ranges, German *Langgrößen*), jackets long enough to cover the seat, higher-rise trousers to keep the waist where it reads natural, no cropped jackets |
| Short torso across men's and women's sizing | balance | women's and unisex tops are often shorter in the body; men's shirts may need shortening; mid-rise trousers, tops untucked or half-tucked, jackets ending at the hip |
| Short neck | lengthen | open necklines, no chokers, small collars (see face-framing) |
| Broad back / muscular arms | room to move | stretch or bias-cut fabric, raglan sleeves, check sleeve circumference when buying; tailoring for sherwanis and suits |

## 5. Frame and scale

- **Fine frame**: small prints, finer fabrics, narrower lapels, delicate jewellery, smaller bags.
- **Medium**: most scales.
- **Broad frame**: medium to large prints, substantial fabrics (twill, heavier silks, brocade),
  bolder jewellery, wider lapels.

## 6. Height

- **Petite** (often under about 160 cm for women and 170 cm for men in sizing, but it's about
  proportion more than a number): one colour or tonal dressing, vertical lines, shorter jackets, V
  necks, fewer horizontal breaks, proportioned prints, petite or tailored hems, cropped trousers that
  show the ankle, lighter lehenga cans-can and fewer tiers, saree with narrow borders and fine pleats
  (Kanjivaram with a slimmer border, or a light silk), kurtas at knee or just above with straight
  pants, heels or platforms only if wanted. Sizes from petite ranges or tailoring.
- **Tall**: can carry long lengths, large prints, wide-leg trousers, maxi lengths, long coats, and
  contrasting top and bottom. Check sleeve and inseam lengths ("tall" ranges). Sarees may need
  6.3 m or longer, or tucking the pleats a little lower. Long kurtas (calf length) and floor-length
  Anarkalis suit tall heights well.
- In heat, tall men do better in unlined two-piece suits than three-piece suits (see section 15).
- For men: petite men benefit from shorter suit jackets that cover the seat, slim lapels, no
  trouser breaks, monochrome; tall men from three-piece suits, bandhgalas with structure, wider
  lapels, and enough sleeve length.

## 7. Fabric and drape

| Fabric quality | Behaves | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Soft drape (chiffon, georgette, crepe, jersey, modal, mulmul) | follows the body, moves | softening, hot climates (natural ones), elegant evening wear, sarees that hug |
| Crisp (cotton poplin, organza, taffeta, linen, tussar, chanderi) | holds its own shape | structure, standing away from the body, hot dry climates (linen, cotton) |
| Heavy / structured (brocade, Kanjivaram and Banarasi silk, velvet, wool, denim, raw silk) | builds shape | formal and bridal, cold weather, broad frames |
| Stretch knits | moves with body | comfort, seated users, pregnancy |
| Bias cut | skims curves | evening wear; can cling, so check fit |

Climate: in humid heat (Chennai, Mumbai, Singapore, Lagos), choose cotton, linen, mulmul,
chanderi, khadi, modal and light silk-cotton; avoid heavy polyester. In dry heat, loose, covering
natural fabrics protect from sun. Cold: wool, cashmere, pashmina, layered silk with thermal under.

## 8. Fit checklist (all genders)

- **Shoulders**: seam at the shoulder bone. This is the hardest thing to alter, so prioritise it when buying.
- **Chest/bust**: no pulling at buttons; blouses should allow lifting the arms.
- **Waist**: one or two fingers of room; seated users need more.
- **Sleeves**: shirts end at the wrist bone; jacket sleeves show about 1 cm of shirt cuff.
- **Trousers**: rise comfortable sitting down; hem at the shoe top (slight break) or ankle.
- **Kurta**: side slits start at the hip; shoulders fit; chest has room.
- **Saree blouse**: fitted at the bust and back, no gaping at the neck, armholes comfortable, padding
  optional; petticoat drawstring at the natural waist or slightly below.
- **Sherwani / bandhgala**: collar closes without pressing the throat; enough room to sit and dance.
- **Shoes**: fit at the end of the day; for long events, a block heel, mojaris with padding or flats.

## 9. Plus size

- Every style is available in plus sizes. Choose by proportion, colour and personality, as for anyone.
- Fit is the difference-maker: well-fitted shoulders and bust, waistbands that don't dig in, and
  fabrics with some weight that move (crepe, georgette, heavier jersey, chanderi, silk-cotton).
- Structure is a friend, not a disguise: tailored jackets, bandhgalas, A-line kurtas, wrap dresses,
  sarees in drapey silks (georgette, crepe, soft silk) or structured ones (Kanjivaram) both work.
- Prints: any scale that matches the person's frame; bold prints look intentional.
- Plus sizes from tailors are common in India, so a blouse or kurta can be tailored to measurement.

## 10. Pregnancy and postpartum

- Comfortable waist (under-bump or over-bump bands), stretch, wrap and empire shapes, front
  openings for feeding after birth, breathable fabrics, flat or low shoes.
- Sarees work well in pregnancy: tie the petticoat below the bump, use a light fabric, keep the
  pleats loose; wear a looser or stretch blouse. Anarkalis and A-line kurtas with stretch leggings,
  kaftans and abayas are also comfortable.
- Never comment on weight or "bouncing back".

## 11. Adaptive and seated dressing

Standard clothing is cut for standing bodies. Sources from rehabilitation and adaptive-fashion
communities agree on these points:
- **Measure seated** for waist, hip, thigh and inseam.
- **Trousers**: higher back rise and lower front rise, no back pockets or thick seams (pressure
  areas), side zips or magnetic or hook-and-loop openings, stretch, longer leg length so the hem
  reaches the shoe when seated, wide or stretch legs over braces.
- **Tops**: shorter at the front to avoid bunching; longer at the back; wrap, front-opening and
  magnetic-button shirts; raglan sleeves for movement; no long fabric near the wheels.
- **Outerwear**: short jackets, capes, ponchos; no long coats that catch in the wheels.
- **Skirts and sarees**: a skirt cut a little longer at the front sits evenly when seated. A pre-
  stitched (ready-to-wear) saree, a lehenga-style saree or a skirt-and-dupatta keeps the look without
  long pleating. Keep the pallu light and pinned so it can't catch in the wheels.
- **Closures**: magnetic buttons, large zip pulls, hook-and-loop, elastic laces, slip-on shoes,
  pre-tied ties and turbans where religious practice allows.
- **Sensory needs**: tagless labels, flat seams, seamless knits, soft fabrics; avoid scratchy
  embellishment on the inside (line blouse embroidery with soft cotton).
- **Dressing with a carer**: dress the less mobile side first, undress it last.
- **Prosthetics and limb differences**: wider leg openings, stretch, side zips; one-handed closures;
  ask which side needs access.
- Seated proportions: the person's upper body and lap are what others see, so focus colour,
  neckline and jewellery on the top half, and choose fabrics that look smooth when seated (matte
  crepe, structured cotton, knits).

## 12. Older adults

- Hair greying: silver or white hair changes contrast, and the direction depends on skin depth. On light skin it lowers contrast and often moves the palette softer or cooler; on medium-deep and deep skin it raises contrast and supports clearer, richer colour near the face. Re-check with two questions: does crisp white or cream look better now, and do bold colours look more alive or louder than before?
- Prioritise comfort, easy closures, warmth and non-slip shoes, with no loss of style.
- Skin can become more sensitive, so choose soft natural fabrics.
- Avoid assuming "age-appropriate" rules; ask what the person enjoys.
- Jewellery with limited grip: magnetic or large lobster clasps, stretch or open-cuff bangles
  instead of tight bangles over the knuckles, lightweight earrings with comfortable backs, long
  chains that go over the head without a clasp.

## 13. Gender-neutral and gender-affirming fit

- Ask which garment families and pronouns to use. Don't assume.
- Gender-neutral silhouettes: straight or relaxed trousers, overshirts, boxy or relaxed tailoring,
  long kurtas with straight pants, jumpsuits, knitwear, coats, kimono-style jackets, Nehru jackets.
- Fit across "men's" and "women's" sizing: men's shirts fit broader shoulders and longer torsos;
  women's tailoring suits narrower shoulders and a defined waist; unisex ranges sit between. Tailors
  can adapt any of these.
- Gender-affirming goals the person may name (broader shoulders, a less defined waist, softer
  shoulders, more defined hips): use structure, layering, shoulder seams, rise and pattern
  placement. Binders and shapewear are the person's own choice; mention comfort and breaks only if
  they bring them up.

## 14. Optional lenses (use only if the person asks)

- **Kibbe** (David Kibbe's image identities: Dramatic, Natural, Classic, Gamine, Romantic, with
  sub-types) mixes bone structure, flesh and essence. It is popular but hard to assign from photos.
  Offer it as a lens to explore, not a verdict.
- **Japanese 骨格診断 (kokkaku shindan) three frames**: *Straight* (compact, dimensional upper body,
  firm texture: suits simple, structured, crisp fabrics), *Wave* (softer, lower body more prominent,
  fine bones: suits soft fabrics, defined waist, lighter details), *Natural* (larger visible bone
  structure, frame-forward: suits relaxed, oversized, textured fabrics). Popular in Japan and Korea.
- **"Fruit" or letter shapes** (apple, pear, hourglass, rectangle, inverted triangle): many people
  find these labels unkind. Translate to the proportions in section 4 instead.

## 15. Menswear tailoring and alterations (suits, bandhgalas, sherwanis, any wearer)

- **Shoulders decide the jacket.** The seam should end at the shoulder bone with no divot or
  overhang. Narrow or sloping shoulders: light natural padding or a structured shoulder; avoid
  extended or heavily roped shoulders. Broad shoulders: natural shoulder with enough room across the
  back.
- **What a tailor can do easily**: take in or let out the waist (within seam allowance), shorten
  sleeves and trousers, taper trousers, shorten a kurta. **Hard or not worth it**: narrow the
  shoulders, lengthen a jacket body, fix a collar that stands away from the neck. A jacket that is
  big in the shoulders is usually better replaced or rented than altered.
- **Proportions**: lapel width and tie width match each other and the frame (narrower on fine
  frames, medium on most); jacket length covers the seat; sleeves show about 1 cm of shirt cuff.
- **Collars**: a long neck or long face suits a higher, wider-spread collar; a short neck or round
  face suits a point or narrower spread collar, top button open when the code allows.
- **Trouser break**: none or slight for a clean modern line (good for shorter legs); a slight
  break for tall men and traditional business settings.
- **Heat and summer events**: half-lined or unlined two-piece suits in linen, cotton or tropical
  wool; three-piece suits and heavy lining are for cool weather.
- **Lead times**: allow about a week for simple alterations and longer before festivals and wedding
  season; book rentals early.

## 16. Plus-size formal and gown fit

- **Support built in**: full-bust wearers usually do best with structure inside the dress, such
  as built-in cups, a corset or boned bodice, or a well-fitted strapless-capable bra. Strapless
  without internal structure can slip.
- **Boning and sitting**: boning should stop above the hip bone so the wearer can sit through a
  dinner; do one fitting **seated**.
- **Skirts**: A-line, trumpet and column all work; mermaid and trumpet need enough room at the knee to
  sit and climb steps; slits help movement.
- **Fabric**: crepe, heavier satin, mikado, lace on a lining, velvet; very thin stretch satin shows
  every seam.
- **Tailoring is normal**: made-to-measure or adjusted gowns are standard in many places (Lagos,
  India, the Gulf); allow time for two fittings.

## 17. Limited grip, carer-assisted and seated dressing details

- **Arthritis and limited grip**: magnetic or large flat buttons, wide zip pulls (add a ring),
  hook-and-loop closures, elastic waists with a flat front, front openings instead of back, kurtas
  and blouses that open fully at the front, pre-tied ties and pre-pleated drapes.
- **With a carer**: front or side openings, no tiny hooks at the back, garments that can be put on
  seated or lying down; dress the less mobile side first.
- **Seated saree** (pre-stitched or carefully draped): tie the petticoat at a comfortable seated
  height, not tight; cut or drape the front slightly longer and the back slightly shorter so the hem
  sits evenly; keep the hem clear of the footrests; keep the pallu short and light, pinned at the
  shoulder and again at the waist so it can't reach the wheels; use a front-opening blouse. A
  ready-to-wear kasavu or cotton saree is easiest.
- **Pins and jewellery**: avoid pins and brooches on the front if the person will hold a baby or be
  hugged; choose smooth jewellery without sharp edges.
- **Sensitive skin with zari or embroidery**: line the blouse and borders with soft cotton, choose
  thin zari borders or cotton kasavu, or wear a soft cotton layer under heavier silk.
