# Occasions and dress codes

Written 2026-11. Dress codes differ by country, venue, host and generation. Read the invitation, ask
the host when unsure, and treat this file as common practice.

## 1. How to read a dress code

Check five things: **the invite wording**, **the venue** (garden, banquet hall, temple, beach),
**time** (day or evening), **culture of the hosts**, and **the person's role** (guest, family,
speaker, bride or groom). When the invite is silent, dress one step above what you think.

## 2. Western dress-code ladder (most formal first)

| Code | Typical menswear | Typical womenswear | Gender-neutral options |
|---|---|---|---|
| White tie | tailcoat, white waistcoat and bow tie | floor-length gown, gloves optional | formal tailcoat or gown |
| Black tie | dinner suit (tuxedo), black bow tie | floor-length gown or very formal cocktail dress, elegant jumpsuit | tuxedo with a blouse, column gown, velvet suit |
| Black tie optional / formal | dark suit and tie or tuxedo | long or cocktail dress, dressy suit | dark suit, dressy jumpsuit |
| Cocktail | suit, tie optional | knee to midi dress, dressy separates | tailored suit, silk shirt |
| Business formal | dark suit, tie | suit or sheath dress with jacket | dark suit |
| Business casual / smart casual | chinos, shirt, blazer, loafers | trousers or skirt, blouse or knit, blazer | trousers, knit, overshirt or blazer |
| Casual | jeans, polo, tee | jeans, tee, sundress | anything neat |

Equivalents in Indian dress: black tie ≈ bandhgala, sherwani or achkan; silk saree, lehenga or
Anarkali. Business formal ≈ bandhgala or suit; silk or handloom saree, kurta suit with dupatta.
Smart casual ≈ kurta with trousers, Nehru jacket; cotton saree, straight kurta with pants.

## 3. Work: interviews and offices by region

| Place | Interviews (default) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| India, corporate | suit or formal shirt and trousers; or a handloom/silk saree, or a straight kurta with trousers and a dupatta | IT and start-ups are more relaxed; banks and law firms more formal |
| South Korea (Seoul) | navy or charcoal suit, white or light blue shirt, plain tie (or no tie for some firms), polished shoes, tidy natural grooming | conservative by default; creative, gaming and start-up firms are more relaxed, so check the company's own photos |
| Japan | black or navy suit (job-hunting suit), white shirt | very conservative norms for new graduates |
| China | dark suit, conservative | tech firms vary |
| Gulf states | suit, or national dress for citizens; modest dress for all | modest coverage expected |
| UK, Europe | suit for finance and law; smart casual for tech and creative | — |
| USA, Canada | business formal to smart casual by sector | — |
| Nigeria, Ghana | suit or polished native wear (well-cut kaftan, senator wear for men; tailored ankara or a suit for women) depending on employer | — |
| Latin America | suits in finance; guayabera in Caribbean heat | — |
| Southeast Asia | suits; batik shirts formal in Malaysia and Indonesia; barong in the Philippines for formal events | heat: lighter fabrics |

Interview colours: navy, charcoal and mid-grey are safe everywhere; choose the shirt or blouse and
tie from the person's palette (the colour nearest the face matters most).

## 5. Galas, black tie and "native" formal

- Black tie (global): see the ladder. Jewel tones, velvet, silk and satin read festive.
- **Lagos galas and owambe events**: invitations often say "black tie" or "corporate / native".
  Expect high glamour: couture lace, aso oke, sequins and beading, gele styled by a professional,
  agbada for men, matching shoes and bags. Tailoring is the norm. Bold colour is welcome.
- Gulf galas: modest evening gowns, embellished abayas or kaftans; men in national dress or tuxedo.
- Indian galas: bandhgala, sherwani, saree, lehenga, gown or Indo-western.

## 6. Graduations and convocations

- Under an academic gown, only the collar, sleeves, hem and shoes show. Choose a collar or neckline
  and a hijab or headwear colour from the palette; wear comfortable closed shoes for walking up
  stairs and stages.
- Modest graduation: long dress, maxi skirt or wide trousers with a long top or abaya under the gown;
  a hijab pinned so the cap can sit on top (bring pins).
- Many Indian universities now use Indian-style robes or stoles, sometimes with a saree, kurta or
  khadi dress code; check the convocation notice.
- Japan: women often wear hakama with a kimono at graduation.

## 7. Religious places and ceremonies

- Hindu temples: covered shoulders and knees; some South Indian temples require veshti for men
  (shirt off at some shrines) and saree or salwar for women; leather may be discouraged.
- Gurdwaras: everyone covers their head, shoes off.
- Mosques: covered arms and legs; women cover hair; shoes off.
- Churches: modest; covered shoulders in many Catholic and Orthodox churches.
- Buddhist temples: covered shoulders and knees; shoes off.
- Synagogues: modest; kippah for men in many.

## 8. Funerals

- Western: dark, plain. Hindu: white or off-white is common. Chinese, Korean: black and white;
  avoid red. Ghana: black, red, or white for an older person's life celebration. Muslim: modest,
  plain. Ask the family when unsure.

## 9. Family events, festivals, parties

- Family events (birthdays, anniversaries, naming ceremonies): comfortable, festive, modesty as the
  family expects; older relatives often appreciate traditional dress.
- Festivals: see `regional-wear.md` part A8 and the country tables.
- House parties and dinners: smart casual.

## 10. Day vs night, indoor vs outdoor, climate

- **Day**: lighter colours and fabrics, less shine, cotton, linen, chanderi, organza.
- **Night**: deeper colours, shine and embellishment, silk, velvet, sequins.
- **Outdoor**: shoes for the ground (grass, sand, uneven), sun protection, layers for evening.
- **Hot humid**: natural breathable fabrics, loose fits, lighter colours, sweat-friendly prints.
- **Cold**: layering under traditional wear (thermals under sarees and kurtas), shawls, long coats.
- **Rain**: shorter hems, quick-dry fabrics, no suede, dark prints.

## 11. Creative workplaces and gender-neutral dressing

- Creative offices reward a point of view: one distinctive element (colour, texture, a statement
  jacket, interesting shoes) with clean basics.
- Gender-neutral wardrobe: straight or wide trousers, relaxed tailoring, overshirts, knitwear,
  long kurtas or tunics, jumpsuits, boots or loafers, unisex jewellery. Fit adjustments: shoulders,
  sleeve and trouser length, rise.
- Interviews in creative fields: polished version of the team's own style.

## 12. Adaptive notes for events

- Long events: seated comfort (stretch, soft waistbands, no back pockets or heavy embellishment
  where the person sits), easy closures, a shawl for air conditioning.
- Wheelchair users: no long trains or long dupattas near wheels; pre-stitched sarees, lehenga
  sarees, kurtas with side openings, short jackets or capes; focus detail on the upper half.
- Older guests: non-slip shoes, a stole for warmth, garments that open at the front.

## 13. Event details the persona tests asked for

**Filter first.** Colours named in this file (sand, olive, pastels, black) are what people commonly
wear, not what suits a given person. Always swap them for the nearest colours in the person's own
palette.

**Garden party / outdoor daytime wedding (Europe and similar):** lighter suits (light grey, soft
blue, stone, or the person's palette equivalents) or a blazer and trousers; tie often optional (ask
the couple); dresses in cotton, silk or crepe at knee to midi length; block heels or flats for
grass; a layer for the evening. In Italy, a common custom treats all-black as too funereal for a
daytime wedding, guests avoid white and ivory, and shoulders are covered in church (bring a jacket
or wrap). Ask the couple when unsure.

**Graduation under a gown:** ask the gown and hood colours (they sit next to the face). Only the
collar, cuffs, hem and shoes show, so put detail there. **Cap over a hijab:** a flat-crown wrap,
a non-slip underscarf, matte fabric, and pins anchored into the underscarf; practise once with a
cap or a book. Plan for the after-event with the gown off (photos, dinner), and in Gulf cities layer
for cold indoor air conditioning and outdoor heat.

**Naming ceremonies and family blessings (Indian and other):** comfortable, festive, traditional if
the family is; the person may hold the baby, so avoid brooches, sharp jewellery and pins on the front;
soft fabrics near the baby's face. Older relatives often appreciate traditional dress (kasavu in
Kerala, silk elsewhere).

**Lagos "black tie" and owambe:** native formal (aso oke, lace, agbada, gele) is accepted and often
expected unless the invitation says otherwise; ask whether there is an aso ebi. Tailors book up
before big events, so allow several weeks for a made-to-measure outfit. Gele and makeup are usually
booked with professionals on the day.

**Dancing events in winter (North Indian sangeet, mehendi):** expect a hot dance floor and a cold
lawn. Breathable inner layers, a removable jacket, shawl or Nehru jacket, no heavy trains, shoes you
can dance in, and pockets or a small bag for the phone.

**Korean job interviews in an existing black suit:** a black suit is acceptable for interviews in
Korea; avoid pairing it with a black tie (reads as funeral dress). A white or light blue shirt and a
navy or subdued patterned tie are safe; fix the shoulder fit or rent if the jacket is too big.
In winter, a plain navy, charcoal or camel overcoat over the suit (taken off before the interview
room). If unsure about a tie at a large company, wear one; it's easy to remove if the room is
tieless, and impossible to add.

**When the person's best colour is the bride's or host's colour:** wear it as a border, blouse,
accent or accessory rather than the main colour.

## 14. Being photographed (events)

- **Face the light**: stand facing windows or the main light; avoid light from directly above.
- **Contrast near the face**: a lighter or brighter colour, a contrast border, or jewellery near the
  face keeps deep and light skin alike from blending into dark backgrounds.
- **Some sheen**: silk, zari and satin catch light; very matte dark fabric on dark skin in dim halls
  can lose detail. Mix matte and shine.
- **Skin**: matte powder against shine, but test for white flashback (some sunscreens and powders).
- **Ask the photographer** to expose for faces; phones in portrait mode do better with the face in
  light. None of this is about looking lighter: the goal is for the camera to see the person.
